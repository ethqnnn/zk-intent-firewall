Everything on the security path is the code in the repository, imported unchanged: packages/mcp-firewall (firewall, prover, verifier), the compiled proof_of_intent circuit, and the dashboard's PasskeyPolicyRegistry. The engine that runs them is sandboxed by Node's permission model: no network at all, no child processes, no file writes. The page asks it to try each of those at boot and shows the result above.

What counts as a break

A genuine break

An unauthorised action that the system accepts as valid. Any one of:

  • The verifier accepts a payment the published policy does not allow: a recipient not on the allowlist (a lookalike, another chain), an amount of zero or over the limit, a tool whose scope is not granted, a time at or after expiry, or a proof under any commitment other than the one published.
  • The firewall issues a proof for such an action.
  • One proof is accepted twice, or for an action other than the one it was made for.
  • The registry accepts a register or revoke approval, or a second passkey enrolment. The owner's key was used twice at boot and then discarded, so no legitimate approval can happen after that.
  • The salt or the policy opening leaks from the service, or the engine reaches the network, a file outside its sandbox, or a process.

The first four are detected automatically by an independent check of every accepted action against the published policy. Those attempts are flagged BREAK in the log, and the counter above turns red.

Not a break (known and documented)

  • In-policy payments. Paying an allowlisted payee up to the limit is allowed by design, even if the agent was tricked into it.
  • Draining by repetition. There is no cumulative budget yet (spec L1); many in-policy payments add up. The treasury tops itself back up.
  • Seeing why you were blocked. In production the agent sees only an opaque refusal; here the operator's reason is shown, because the policy is public anyway.
  • Rate limits and lockouts. Hitting them is the defence working. Exhausting capacity is denial of service, not a break, and please don't.
  • Handles. Names on the leaderboard are self-chosen and unverified.

The full threat model and its limits are in the README and circuit spec (sections 8 and 9).

Attack console

Three ways in. Pick a starting point, edit the JSON, send it. Every attempt is logged publicly, with the check that stopped it. Proofs the firewall hands back are kept here so you can replay, move or tamper with them on the direct route.

From a terminal

The API takes JSON and needs no key. Scripts and other agents are welcome; keep within the rate limits.
